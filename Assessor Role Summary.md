
# Assessor

The **Assessor** is the first person on the ground documenting a home repair site for [ARCHR](http://docs/archr.md). After a [Requestor](http://docs/requestor.md) submits a [home repair assistance application](http://docs/home-repair-assistance-application.md), and during [Eligibility Review](http://docs/eligibility-review.md), a home visit is scheduled. During this visit, in-person contact is established, [Before Photos](http://docs/photos.md) are taken, and any missing [Verification Documents](http://docs/verification.md) can be collected and scanned in person.

---
## Role Description

The Assessor may be a volunteer, a paid independent contractor (similar to a DoorDash or Instacart trip), or a staff member of a partner organization. The role requires basic familiarity with a measuring tape and a smartphone, and exceptional social skills and diplomacy—the Assessor is a guest in someone's home and a representative of the organization.

For organizations hiring solely for this function, common job titles include **Home Inspector**, **Assessment Specialist**, or **Field Technician**.

### Historical note

Early in ARCHR's operation, home inspectors sometimes made three to five trips to a single site before a contract was signed. This was repetitive, laborious, and costly in fuel. The process has since been consolidated into a single comprehensive assessment. For a period, a $200 bounty was offered for complete and comprehensive home damage reports. At the time of writing, assessments are contracted to a third-party firm, and some partner organizations still conduct assessments through staff members.

## Access and Vetting

Assessor access is **automatically available** to [Administrators](http://docs/administrator.md), [Project Managers](http://docs/project-manager.md), and [Envoy](http://docs/envoy.md).

[Volunteers](http://docs/volunteer.md) may be approved for the Assessor role after:

- Passing a short training
- Receiving [volunteer merit](http://docs/volunteer-merit.md)
- Completing a 1:1 interview with an Admin, Manager, or Envoy

Because Assessors enter applicant homes, they are expected to consent to a [Background Check](http://docs/background-check.md) and may be required to sign a [Non-Disclosure Agreement](http://docs/non-disclosure.md).

---
## Minimum Requirements

- Competent at assessing and documenting home repair damage
- Able to take good-quality photos and notes
- Diplomatic, friendly, and sometimes pastoral toward residents
- No criminal or legal record, or documented behavior, that would make them unsafe in a client's home
- Licensed and insured driver, with a professional working vehicle and clean driving record preferred
- Basic technical comfort: mobile scanning and copying, technical photo capture using [Solocator](http://docs/solocator.md), and direct interaction with the [platform](http://docs/platform.md)

---
## Responsibilities

- Schedule and communicate with the homeowner regarding the assessment visit, including disclosing potential travel issues ahead of time
- Take photos of affected sites using [Solocator](http://docs/solocator.md)
- Cross-reference [Application](http://docs/application.md) data against on-the-ground facts, ensuring information on file is accurate
- Collect and copy [income](http://docs/income-verification.md) and [homeownership](http://docs/ownership-verification.md) verification documents when not already provided
- Create a [Scope of Work](http://docs/scope-of-work.md), communicating required repairs and estimated appraised costs
- Create [Repair Tasks](http://docs/repair-task.md)
- Build positive rapport and trust with the homeowner and residents
- Communicate assessment findings to [Staff](http://docs/staff.md) and [Subcontractors](http://docs/subcontractor.md)

### Requirements

- Last Assessment
- Assessment Calendar

- Previous Assessments
- [Messages](http://docs/messages.md)
### Permissions

- Create Assessment
- Upload Photos
- Record Measurements
- Create Tasks
- Take Notes
- Verify Documents
- Update Schedule
### Additional tracking

- Mileage tracking
- Completed assessments count
### Offline capability

The dashboard must support uploading, or drafting, notes and photos when there is no internet connection. If drafts are saved only locally on a phone, there is a risk they will not be submitted.
### Navigation

Directions to and from the property should be available both on the dashboard and in an email alert sent 24 hours before the scheduled assessment.

## Considerations

- Must be able to work offline
- No complex forms
- Guard against [duplicate data entry](http://docs/duplicate-data.md)
- Must be able to upload photos, find and be alerted to appointments, and have mobile accessibility to the system
- [Spanish](http://docs/spanish-language.md) speaking is needed or a strong plus
- Must handle sensitive documents and information with care, respecting [privacy and privilege](http://docs/privacy.md)
- Requestors may request [encrypted communications](http://docs/encrypted-communications.md) if safety is a concern
- Occupational hazards—known or discovered on site—must be documented in notes
### Time and travel

- An assessment, excluding travel, is expected to take between 30 and 60 minutes
- Travel between appointments is significant; the Assessor may need reimbursement for mileage and time

---
## Outcomes

**At its best**, a good assessment means:

- Accurate repair estimates
- Clear task assignments
- Happy clients
- An efficient process

**At its worst**, a poor assessment means:

- The [Scope of Work](http://docs/scope-of-work.md) is wrong
- Confusion on site
- Frustrated clients
- Delays and rework
