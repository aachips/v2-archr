## Who They Are

The Crew Lead is the person on the ground running the repair site. They are responsible for getting the work done safely, on time, and with quality. They manage the crew, coordinate volunteers, and are the primary point of contact with the homeowner during active repairs. The Crew Lead is not an office worker—they are in the field, on their feet, often with limited connectivity.

---

## What They Do (Day to Day)

Supervise the Work  
The Crew Lead oversees all repair activities at their assigned job site. They make sure work is being done correctly, safely, and according to the project scope.

Coordinate the Team  
They manage crew members and volunteers, assigning tasks, tracking hours, and making sure everyone knows what they need to do each day.

Maintain Safety  
Before any work starts, the Crew Lead delivers a safety talk to all crew members and volunteers. This covers the specific hazards of the day's work and any precautions needed. This must be documented.

Document Everything  
The Crew Lead is responsible for keeping a daily log of what happened on site: what work was completed, any issues encountered, and what's planned for tomorrow. They take photos throughout the project—before, during, and after.

Get Signatures  
At the completion of the job, the Crew Lead walks the homeowner through the finished work and gets them to sign the completion certificate.

Report Issues  
If something goes wrong—an accident, a problem with materials, a scope change request—the Crew Lead files the appropriate report and alerts the Project Manager.

---

## What They Need to See on Their Dashboard

Today's Jobs  
A clear list of what's happening today, with addresses, lead time estimates, and a quick glance at the scope. A weather icon shows if the work is indoors or outdoors.

Current Job  
Once on a site, the Crew Lead sees a breakdown of all tasks for that job, their status, estimated time remaining, and a simple way to mark tasks complete as work progresses.

Volunteer Management  
A list of volunteers signed up for the day, with the ability to check them in and confirm they received the safety talk.

Photos and Logs  
An easy way to upload photos directly from the job site and add notes to the daily log.

Messages  
Communications from the Project Manager or other staff that need attention.

## What They Do Differently from Crew Members

|   |   |   |
|---|---|---|
|Task|Crew Lead|Crew Member|
|Safety Talk|Delivers the talk|Attends and signs off|
|Daily Log|Required, daily|Optional|
|Volunteer Sign-off|Verifies hours|Logs own hours|
|Completion Documents|Gets client signature|May witness|
|Incident Reports|Files them|Signs if involved|
|Change Orders|Requests them|Not involved|
|Subcontractors|Coordinates them|Not involved|

## What Would Make Them Frustrated

- Too much typing: They're on a phone. Big forms are painful.
    
- No offline access: Many job sites don't have internet.
    
- Unclear next steps: If they don't know what to do next, work stops.
    
- Missing information: If they don't have the homeowner's contact info or the scope of work, they can't work.
    
- Duplicate data entry: Entering the same information in multiple places wastes valuable time.
    

---

## Success Looks Like

- Work is completed safely and on schedule
    
- Volunteers have a good experience and want to return
    
- Homeowners are happy and sign off without issues
    
- The Project Manager knows what's happening on every site
    
- All documentation is captured in the system without extra work
    

---

One Rule: If it happened on site and matters to the project, log it. Photos, notes, completions, incidents—get it in the system.
