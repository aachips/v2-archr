# Completing a Case & Closing Out

> **Who this is for:** Project Managers doing final inspections, Caseworkers coordinating sign-off, and Bursars reconciling finances.

## Why This Is Final

Proper closure ensures legal protection, grant compliance, and happy applicants. A case that isn't closed properly can come back to haunt you — literally and financially.

**An open case with completed work is a liability.** Funders ask questions. Applicants get confused. Data gets messy. Close it right.

---

## Step 1: Final Inspection Checklist

Before closing a case, the Project Manager should verify:

### Work Completion
- [ ] **All tasks in the project are marked Complete**
- [ ] **Each task has photos** — before and after, clearly labeled
- [ ] **Hours are logged** — volunteer and paid hours both
- [ ] **Materials are recorded** — what was used, what cost
- [ ] **Quality checks passed** — no rework needed

### Property Condition
- [ ] **Worksite is clean** — no debris, tools, or materials left behind
- [ ] **All repairs match scope** — nothing missed, nothing extra (unless change-ordered)
- [ ] **No new damage** — the repair didn't create new problems
- [ ] **Utilities are functional** — water, electricity, HVAC all working

### Documentation
- [ ] **After photos uploaded** — same angles as before photos
- [ ] **Assessment referenced** — the original assessment is linked to the completed work
- [ ] **Progress events logged** — the timeline tells the full story
- [ ] **Change orders documented** — any scope changes have approval records

---

## Step 2: Applicant Sign-Off

The applicant should confirm they're satisfied with the work:

1. **Walk through the repairs** with the applicant
2. **Explain what was done** — in plain language, not construction jargon
3. **Ask for sign-off** — a verbal confirmation at minimum, a signature if your program requires it
4. **Address any concerns** — if the applicant isn't satisfied, note the issue and assign a corrective task

> **What if the applicant isn't happy?** Don't close the case. Assign a follow-up task, fix the issue, and do another walkthrough. A closed case with an unhappy applicant is a complaint waiting to happen.

### Certificate of Completion

Some programs issue a Certificate of Completion to the applicant. This typically includes:
- Household name and address
- Date of completion
- Description of work performed
- Organization name
- Signature of PM or caseworker

This is a nice touch for the applicant and useful for grant documentation.

---

## Step 3: Upload Final After-Photos

After-photos should:
- **Match before-photo angles** — same position, same lighting if possible
- **Show the full repair area** — wide shot plus close-up
- **Be clearly labeled** — "After - Roof repair, east section"
- **Be uploaded to the case** — not just stored on someone's phone

> **Grant reporting tip:** Funders love before-and-after comparisons. Good photos are the difference between "they said they did the work" and "here's the proof."

---

## Step 4: Reconcile Finances

The Bursar should:

1. **Review all invoices** — subcontractor bills, material receipts
2. **Compare to budget** — did the project stay within the allocated amount?
3. **Process final payments** — any outstanding invoices get paid
4. **Record expenses** — all costs logged against the case
5. **Generate a financial summary** — budgeted vs. actual, by category

**If the project went over budget:**
- Document why (change order? under-estimate? unexpected issue?)
- Get admin approval for the overage
- Note the reason in the case file

**If the project came in under budget:**
- Document the savings
- The excess returns to the organization's funding pool

> **Financial accuracy matters.** Grant auditors will look at individual case costs. Every dollar needs a receipt and a reason.

---

## Step 5: Close the Case in the System

When everything above is done:

1. **Open the case** in your dashboard
2. **Verify all projects are marked Complete** — a case can have multiple projects; all must be done
3. **Set case status to "Concluded"** — this signals the case is fully closed
4. **Log the closing event** — "Case concluded on [date] — all repairs completed, applicant satisfied"
5. **Confirm the application status** — if ALL repair needs across ALL cases are met, the Application status should also update to "Concluded"

---

## Step 6: Post-Completion Follow-Up

**30 days after closure:**
- Contact the applicant to confirm repairs are holding up
- Note any issues — roof still leaking? Plumbing working?
- If problems are found, the case can be **reopened** (see below)

**90 days after closure:**
- Final check-in (programs vary on whether this is required)
- Close the loop with the funder if they require post-completion reporting

---

## When Cases Get Reopened

Sometimes a case needs to come back to life. Common reasons:

| Reason | What Happens |
|--------|-------------|
| **Repair failure** — the roof leaks again | Reopen case, assign corrective task, document the failure |
| **New damage** — a storm causes additional damage | This may be a NEW application, not a reopen |
| **Grant audit finding** — funder questions costs | Reopen case, gather documentation, respond to funder |
| **Applicant complaint** — something wasn't done right | Reopen case, assess the complaint, fix if warranted |

**Reopening a case:**
1. Change status from "Concluded" back to "Active"
2. Log the reopening reason
3. Assign corrective tasks
4. Set a timeline for resolution
5. Close again when resolved

---

## Archiving Case Documents

Once a case is concluded and the follow-up period has passed:

- All documents should be **properly filed** in the case record
- Photos should be **organized** (before/after by repair type)
- Financial records should be **complete** (receipts, invoices, payments)
- Communications should be **logged** (all interactions in the system)

**Retention periods vary by funder and program.** Common requirements:
- Financial records: 7 years
- Photos: permanent
- Contracts: 10 years
- General case files: 5 years
