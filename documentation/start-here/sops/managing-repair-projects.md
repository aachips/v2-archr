# Managing Repair Projects

> **Who this is for:** Project Managers who oversee repair work and Crew Leads who manage on-site operations.

## Why This Matters

This is the operational core of the system — turning assessments into completed work. Everything before this point is preparation. Everything after is documentation. **This is where the actual repair happens.**

A well-managed project means the applicant gets their home fixed, the crew does good work, and the funder sees results. A poorly managed project means delays, budget overruns, and a household stuck in limbo.

---

## Step 1: Create Work Orders from the Assessment

Once the assessment is submitted and approved:

1. **Open the case** in your dashboard
2. **Review the assessment** — what repairs are needed?
3. **Create a Project** — give it a name (e.g., "42-Cherry: Roof & Plumbing Repair")
4. **Break repairs into Repair Tasks** — each task is one actionable item

**Task structure:**
- **Task name** — specific and actionable ("Replace damaged shingles on east roof section")
- **Assigned trade** — roofing, plumbing, electrical, etc.
- **Assigned to** — crew member, subcontractor, or volunteer team
- **Estimated hours** — from the assessment
- **Materials needed** — list what's required
- **Priority** — urgent, normal, or low

> **Rule of thumb:** If two different people could work on it independently, it should be two separate tasks. "Fix the bathroom" is a project. "Replace subfloor," "Install new vanity," and "Repair shower surround" are tasks.

---

## Step 2: Assign Tasks

### To Crew Members
Assign tasks to your in-house crew when:
- The work is within their skill set
- You have availability in their schedule
- The task doesn't require specialized licensing (electrical, plumbing may need licensed subs)

### To Subcontractors
Assign to subcontractors when:
- The work requires a licensed professional
- The task is outside your crew's capabilities
- You need specialized equipment

### To Volunteers
Assign to volunteers when:
- The task is volunteer-friendly (demo, painting, cleanup)
- Supervision is available (crew lead on-site)
- Safety risks are minimal

> **Important:** Volunteers should never be assigned tasks requiring licensed work, working at heights, or handling hazardous materials.

---

## Step 3: Material Requests

If tasks require materials:

1. **Create a material request** in the platform
2. **List items needed** — be specific (not "lumber" but "2×4×8 pressure-treated, 12 pieces")
3. **Submit for approval** — PM or admin approves the request
4. **Track delivery** — note when materials arrive on-site

**Best practice:** Order materials before the crew starts that task. Nothing kills momentum like "we can't do the work because the materials haven't arrived."

---

## Step 4: Track Progress on the Dashboard

Your dashboard should show:
- **Tasks by status** — pending, in progress, complete, blocked
- **Tasks by assignee** — who's working on what
- **Budget status** — how much has been spent vs. allocated
- **Timeline** — start date, estimated completion, actual progress

**Update tasks daily:**
- Mark tasks **In Progress** when work begins
- Mark tasks **Complete** when finished
- Add **photos** of completed work
- Log **hours worked** — this matters for grant reporting and volunteer recognition

> **If you didn't log it, it didn't happen.** Funders need to see hours, costs, and outcomes. Your dashboard data becomes their report.

---

## Step 5: Handle Change Orders

Sometimes the scope changes. The crew opens a wall and finds mold. The assessment missed something. The applicant has an additional need.

**Change order process:**
1. **Document the change** — what's different from the original assessment?
2. **Estimate the impact** — additional cost, time, materials
3. **Get approval** — PM or admin approves the change
4. **Update the project** — add new tasks, adjust budget, revise timeline
5. **Notify the applicant** — they should know what changed and why

> **Never do unapproved extra work.** If it's not in the project scope and hasn't been approved through a change order, it doesn't get done. This protects the organization, the funder, and the applicant.

---

## Step 6: Coordinate Multiple Trades

When multiple trades work on the same property:

1. **Sequence matters** — electricians before drywall, plumbers before flooring
2. **Communicate the schedule** — everyone should know when they're on-site
3. **Designate a site lead** — someone on-site to coordinate (usually the crew lead)
4. **Log handoffs** — when one trade finishes and another begins, note it

**Common sequencing:**
1. Emergency/stabilization first
2. Structural repairs
3. Roof and exterior envelope
4. Plumbing and electrical rough-in
5. Insulation and drywall
6. Finish work (painting, flooring, fixtures)
7. Final inspection and cleanup

---

## Step 7: Quality Control Checkpoints

At key milestones, verify the work:

| Checkpoint | What to Verify |
|-----------|----------------|
| **After demo** | All damaged material removed, structural issues visible |
| **After rough-in** | Plumbing/electrical/HVAC installed correctly before walls close |
| **After finish** | Work meets quality standards, no visible defects |
| **Final walkthrough** | Everything in scope is complete, site is clean, applicant is satisfied |

**If something doesn't pass:** Note the issue, assign a corrective task, and re-inspect when complete. Don't close a task that isn't actually done.

---

## Step 8: Mark Tasks Complete and Close Work Orders

When all tasks in a project are complete:

1. **Verify each task** — photos uploaded, hours logged, materials recorded
2. **Do a final walkthrough** — with the applicant if possible
3. **Mark the project Complete** in the platform
4. **Log the completion event** — "Project [name] completed on [date]"
5. **Trigger the closeout process** — invoice reconciliation, after photos, applicant sign-off

## Quick Reference

| Action | Where | Who |
|--------|-------|-----|
| Create project | Case detail → "New Project" | PM, Crew Lead |
| Add tasks | Project detail → "Add Task" | PM, Crew Lead |
| Assign tasks | Task detail → "Assign To" | PM, Crew Lead |
| Request materials | Project detail → "Material Request" | Crew Lead |
| Update task status | Task detail → Status dropdown | Anyone working on task |
| Log hours | Task detail → "Log Hours" | Crew, Volunteers |
| Add photos | Task detail → "Upload Photos" | Anyone on-site |
| Approve change order | Project detail → "Change Order" | PM, Admin |
| Close project | Project detail → "Mark Complete" | PM |
